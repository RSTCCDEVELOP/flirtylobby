1. Introduction

easy2edit is a simple, modular management system (CMS) for portals, websites, webshops and communities. It is built on a Django (REST) backend and a Next.js frontend, which together keep the system fast and reliable while allowing a flexible, modern way of working.

Rather than starting from a fixed template, you build a website from blocks and switch modules and plugins on or off, depending on what your site needs. This means you only use what you actually need, and you can grow your site over time by adding more functionality when required.

What makes easy2edit modular#

The modular approach is at the heart of easy2edit. Instead of one rigid package, the system is made up of smaller building blocks that you can combine:

  • Modules are parts of the system that can be switched on or off. They are managed in Super admin > Modules.
  • Plugins are signed extension packages that bring their own pages and modules. They are managed in Admin > Plugins.

This allows a site to stay lean when only basic functionality is needed, and to expand easily when new features, pages or tools are required.

Who uses easy2edit#

There are three kinds of people who work with the system, each with a different level of access:

  • Users are visitors with an account. They use the site, for example as customers, members or community participants.
  • Admins manage parts of the site. Their rights come from the roles assigned to them, so one admin might manage only content, while another manages a webshop or a community area.
  • Superadmins are the owner or reseller of the site. They have access to everything, including system settings, modules, plugins, prices and the licence.

This structure means that day-to-day management can be safely delegated to admins, while full control over the system stays with the superadmin.

The dashboard#

After logging in, users, admins and superadmins work from the dashboard. It is organised into three areas:

  • Dashboard: your own profile, notifications and your own AI credit.
  • Admin: the management area, where admins configure and maintain parts of the site.
  • Super admin: the system area, reserved for superadmins, where system-wide settings, modules, plugins, prices and the licence are managed.

Which of these areas you see, and what you can do inside them, depends on whether you are a user, an admin or a superadmin.

Standard installations#

To make it easy to start a new site, easy2edit offers standard installations. With one click, a standard installation sets the modules, plugins and theme for a particular type of website, such as a standard website, web shop, company website, business network, intranet, dating platform, sports club, foundation, marketplace or game hall.

Each installation is shown as a small tile with a settings pop-up. This pop-up is visible in the admin area and is also offered the first time an installation is set up. It lets you configure AI credit settings, modules, plugins and demo data for that type of site.

The settings pop-up has five tabs:

  • General
  • AI
  • Modules
  • Plugins
  • Demo data

On the Plugins tab, a superadmin has a switch for every plugin and every module belonging to that installation. Any change made here is saved immediately as the standard for the next time this type of installation is set up, and it is written to the installations file (backend/apps/core/installations.json) that other sites receive with an update. An administrator sees the same list, but read-only, so they can check the configuration without being able to change it.

Two modules are always part of every site and cannot be switched off: Energy & emissions and Energy: collector module. These are core modules.

The header#

At the top of the screen, the header gives quick access to a few general controls:

  • The language switcher, to change the display language.
  • The edit-mode switch (pencil icon), to turn editing of the page on or off.
  • Light/dark mode, to switch the visual theme.
  • The user menu, for account-related actions.

Getting help#

Help is always close at hand. On every page of the Admin and Super admin areas, a Help button (a question mark icon, next to the back button) opens this manual in a pop-up, directly at the chapter that belongs to the page you are viewing.

On the Admin start page, under About easy2edit, you will find two large buttons that give access to the full manual: one opens the HTML manual, either in a pop-up or in a new tab, and the other offers a PDF download. The manual itself is written in English.
