6. Users, roles and security

easy2edit gives you fine control over who can access your site, what they are allowed to do once they are in, and how the site protects itself and its visitors. This chapter explains users, user groups, roles and the security tools available in the admin area.

Video: Users, roles and security (English voice-over also in Nederlands)

Users#

Every person who needs access to the admin area or a personal presence on the website has an account under Users. From here you can:

  • Create new accounts
  • Block accounts temporarily
  • Delete accounts that are no longer needed

Each user account can have a profile photo and an openbaar profile, which can be shown on the website. This is useful for authors, moderators or team members who should be visible to visitors.

User groups#

If you manage many accounts, it is often easier to work with user groups rather than individual users. A user group is a collection of users that share the same roles and the same module access. Instead of configuring rights for each person separately, you assign rights once to the group, and every member automatically receives them.

This is particularly helpful for teams such as editors, moderators or support staff, where everyone in the team needs the same level of access.

Roles and rights#

Roles (Rollen) define what a user or group is allowed to do inside the admin area. A role is simply a named set of rights, for example:

  • Content manager
  • Moderator
  • View AI use of users

Each right unlocks a specific part of the admin area. By combining rights into roles, you can tailor access precisely to what a team member needs, without giving them full administrative control.

Superadmins always have every right, regardless of the roles configured. If the Rollen module itself is switched off, only superadmins have any rights at all, so make sure this module stays enabled once you start delegating work to other staff.

Module access per user or group#

In addition to roles, individual modules can be switched on or off for a specific user or group. This is managed under Super admin > Modules, using the person icon. This gives you an extra layer of control: even if someone has a role with broad rights, you can still decide exactly which modules they are allowed to use.

Dashboard and profile#

Once logged in, every user lands on their dashboard, where they can manage their own profile. This is also where the login process itself takes place. Encourage users to keep their profile information and photo up to date, especially if their profile is shown publicly on the site.

General security settings#

Under Algemene beveiliging you configure the baseline security behaviour for the whole site:

  • Inlogslot (login lock) to stop repeated failed login attempts
  • Sessieduur (session time) to control how long a user stays logged in
  • Wachtwoordregels (password rules) to enforce stronger passwords
  • Beveiligingsheaders (security headers) to strengthen the technical protection of the site

Review these settings early on, since they form the foundation of your site's overall security posture.

Two-step verification#

Two-step verification adds a second check in addition to the password when logging in. By default, it is required for all admins and superadmins, ensuring that the most powerful accounts are the best protected.

If someone is blocked because of a problem with two-step verification, an email is automatically sent to the administrator, so access issues can be resolved quickly.

Country control#

The Landcontrole module lets you block or allow access to the website based on the visitor's country, using IP-based checks. This is useful if your content or services should only be available in certain regions.

Age check and gambling warning#

Two modules help you meet age-related legal requirements:

  • Leeftijdscontrole shows an 18+ popup to visitors before they can view the site.
  • Gokken-popup shows a combined age and warning popup specifically for gambling-related content.

Enable whichever of these applies to your website's content.

Cookies#

The Cookies module manages the cookie banner shown to visitors, their saved preferences, and a cookie policy popup. This helps you present cookie information clearly and keep a record of visitor consent.

Word filter#

The Woordfilter module lets you define a list of forbidden words. These words are then automatically checked for in texts, names and captions submitted on the site, helping you keep user-generated content appropriate.

Practical tips#

  • Start by setting up roles and user groups before creating many individual accounts, so new users can be added quickly with the right access.
  • Keep two-step verification enabled for all admin and superadmin accounts.
  • Review login lock, session time and password rules together, since they work hand in hand to protect accounts.
  • Turn on age checks, gambling warnings or country control only if they are relevant to your content, to avoid unnecessary friction for visitors.
